Niwaki (庭木) are Japan's garden trees, sculpted by meticulous pruning so that every branch serves the form of the whole.

This project applies that spirit to Mixture-of-Experts language models: open weights pruned to fit the memory you have, measured against the unpruned model, and released for MLX and llama.cpp.

Latest release

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next 141B-A6B Niwaki v4.0 · lattice-coded experts

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next at 141B total and about 5.9B active parameters, its routed experts stored as E8 lattice codes at about 3, 1.4 or 1 bit per weight: 44.8 GB on disk, the same as v2.4, for 64 GB Macs. In that space it keeps more of the model's experts than v2.4 — 17,259 of 24,576 — and reads 10% lower perplexity. The task average reaches 0.794 against the unpruned model's 0.759, the program-state behaviours we test stay at the unpruned model's level, and twenty languages sit within 0.96–1.17× of its perplexity.

Perplexity over 64 × 2048-token windows; task average over arc_easy, hellaswag, piqa, winogrande and boolq, zero-shot, 500 examples each, the same examples for every row; languages on FLORES-200 devtest (English, Spanish, French, German, Russian, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Arabic and Hindi), 1.00 meaning the unpruned model's perplexity; the program-state suite (variable tracking, distractor lines, two-step arithmetic, multi-hop lookups) scored by forced choice on two held-out seeds, and the arithmetic figure on 2,000 freshly generated problems scored the same way. The task average sits above the unpruned model's on the strength of boolq, winogrande and arc_easy; on hellaswag and piqa v4.0 stays below it. v2.4 stays lower on Hindi (0.97 against 1.12) and Malayalam; on the other eighteen of the twenty languages we measure, v4.0 reads lower. The best 2-bit cast of the unpruned model (routed experts and n-gram table at 2-bit) is 10.5 GB larger than v4.0 and behind it on both perplexity and task average. In MLX, v4.0 decodes 2–3% slower than v2.4, and its prompt throughput on 2,048 tokens is 9–13% lower. Protocols and the full tables are on the model cards.

In llama.cpp

The v4.0 file stores the same experts as the MLX weights at higher precision, Q3_K and IQ4_NL, which makes it 17 GB larger; on identical tokens it reads 9% lower perplexity than they do. On an M4 Max with Metal (llama-bench, in one session with v2.4's UD-Q3K file) it runs at 506 tokens/s on a 512-token prompt, 396 on a 2,048-token prompt and 26 in decode, against 488, 417 and 26 for v2.4. On a Mac it holds 42.0 GiB on the GPU at a 2,048-token context, within a 64 GB Mac's default GPU limit; its n-gram table stays in CPU memory, read from the file as tokens need it. The 23GB file is the GGUF build of the 19 GB model below and holds 21.8 GiB on the GPU. Like the v2.4 files, these need the neopolita-llama.cpp fork: stock llama.cpp cannot represent this model's layout yet.

For 32 GB Macs: v4.0 · 19 GB

The v4.0 model sized for 32 GB Macs: its routed experts are stored as E8 lattice codes at about 2 bits per weight, and recovery training follows. Its 14.4 GB n-gram table stays on disk and is read as tokens need it, so the model holds about 19 GB in memory although the download is 33.3 GB. Against v2.4's 102B, the smallest v2.4 release, it reads lower perplexity and KL divergence, a higher task average and an equal or higher score on every program-state behaviour.

  • 19.1 GiBpeak on a 2,048-token prompt, under a 32 GB Mac's default GPU limit of 21.3 GiB
  • −17%WikiText-2 perplexity against v2.4's 102B (6.51 against 7.84)
  • 0.765task average, against 0.738 for v2.4's 102B and 0.759 for the unpruned model
  • 92.9%of 2,000 fresh two-step arithmetic problems, against 98.0% for the unpruned model
  • MLX weights ↗33.3 GB download · Apple silicon, 32 GB

From Qwen3.6-35B-A3B

Qwen3.6 30B · 27B · 24B Niwaki v3.0 · per-expert precision

Qwen3.6-35B-A3B with each of its 10,240 routed experts stored at its own precision — 4, 3 or 2 bits — or removed, in three sizes that all run in 24 GB of unified memory. The 30B holds the 8-bit reference's task average at 3.4× less expert storage: perplexity 3–8% above the reference's, program-state tracking at its level, and ten major languages within 4–21% of it.

Perplexity over WikiText-2's full test set in 2048-token windows; task average over the complete arc_easy, hellaswag, piqa, winogrande and boolq test sets; languages on FLORES-200 devtest (English, Spanish, French, German, Russian, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Arabic and Hindi), 1.00 meaning the reference's perplexity; arithmetic ranges over two evaluation seeds. Read it as a split on one family: v3.0 is ahead of v2.3 on everything here except two-step arithmetic in generation, where v2.3's layout stays above the reference — pick v2.3 for exact multi-step arithmetic, v3.0 for everything else. Languages beyond these ten are not protected the same way.

In llama.cpp

Each GGUF file reads within 0.5% of its MLX model on identical tokens, in English and in each of the ten languages. On an M4 Max with Metal (llama-bench, 512-token prompt, 128 generated tokens) the v3.0 files run at 936–978 tokens/s on the prompt and 63–64 tokens/s in decode, against 82 for v2.3's UD-Q3K build; each v3.0 layer runs its experts in two precision tiers, which costs speed against v2.3's single-precision files. The unpruned model's dynamic quants of similar size decoded at 41–43 tokens/s in an earlier session. The files need the neopolita-llama.cpp fork at tag v3.0 or later: stock llama.cpp cannot represent per-expert precision yet.
